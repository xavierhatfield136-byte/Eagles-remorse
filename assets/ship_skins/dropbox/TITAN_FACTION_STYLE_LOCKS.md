# Titan Faction Style Locks

Use one of these faction style locks together with a faction Titan `HULL_PROMPTS.md` file when generating Titan or Mothership hulls in ChatGPT.

## Blue Allied Expeditionary Navy

Use the visual language of a heroic blue allied expeditionary navy returning home with the largest ships it has ever built. These hulls should feel human-made, disciplined, and militarized, but more prestigious and more monumental than the current blue fleet. The family language should come from strong central spines, broad armored shoulders, layered prow geometry, protected command decks, integrated hangar or service architecture where appropriate, and a clean continuous silhouette that reads as a true warship from far away.

Dominant materials should be cool naval steel, slate blue armor, pale gray plating, restrained cobalt identification fields, and small cyan command or sensor lights. Blue should read as painted armor and fleet markings, not as glowing energy seams across the whole hull. Use light gray and steel as the main hull body, with blue concentrated on armor fields, command sections, recessed stripe panels, and faction markings. A few warm white windows or command-glass zones are fine, but do not turn the ships into civilian liners.

These are Titan and Mothership hulls, so every silhouette should read as a super-capital ship first. Avoid aircraft wings, jet fuselages, tailfins, anime mecha shapes, floating detached ring parts, and giant exposed cannons. Weapon identity should be implied through hull architecture, prow structure, spinal mass, shield housings, bay framing, or service modules rather than visible turrets or missiles. Keep everything in true top-down orthographic sprite language with a transparent background and a crisp alpha silhouette.

The Titans should look like different branches of one blue fleet family: same manufacturing culture, same paint discipline, same high-end naval finish, but each hull class should have a distinct battlefield job. The Mothership should be the apex of the whole family: part fleet anchor, part protected mobile harbor, part command citadel, unmistakably larger and more important than any Titan.

## Red Kinetic Navy

Use the visual language of a disciplined red kinetic navy that believes wars are won by armor, gunnery, and command pressure rather than spectacle. These Titan and Mothership hulls should feel human-made, militarized, and severe: dense armored spines, broad brutal shoulders, layered prow geometry, strong centerline mass, protected command decks, and continuous super-capital silhouettes that read as fortress warships from far away.

Dominant materials should be blackened steel, dark gunmetal, charcoal armor, deep crimson command and identification fields, restrained warning-stripe reds, and small practical white or amber utility lights. Red should read as painted armor sectors and tactical markings, not as bright glowing seams. Keep the hull body mostly steel and dark iron, with red concentrated on command zones, armor belts, recessed panel blocks, and military ID plates.

These are super-capital warships, so avoid aircraft wings, jet fuselages, tailfins, anime mecha shapes, floating detached ring parts, and giant exposed cannons. Weapon identity should be implied through prow structure, armored spinal mass, bay framing, shield-housing bulk, recoil-like hull rhythm, and siege architecture rather than visible guns or missiles. Keep everything top-down orthographic sprite language, transparent background, crisp alpha silhouette, and one contiguous hull.

The family language should connect all red Titans as one fleet culture: disciplined line-battle construction, practical armor logic, severe industrial naval finishing, and heavy kinetic-warship authority. The red Mothership should be the apex of that family: part command citadel, part armored fleet anchor, part mobile war-harbor, unmistakably larger and more important than any Titan.

## Green Aegis Lattice

Use the visual language of the existing Team C green fleet already established in the project, then scale it up into Titans and a Mothership using the approved hull-only references in `green_team_c_titans/approved/ship_hull_cutouts.zip`. Ignore the loose JPG and PNG files beside that zip; those are test images with bad backgrounds and should not define the style. These super-capitals are not blue allied ships recolored green, and they are not generic white cathedral ships. They should read as pale celadon, sea-glass, mint-silver, ivory-green, and silver-green shield warships with deep emerald and teal glazing buried inside the hull, brighter and greener than the blue faction with calm pale armor and controlled dark recesses.

The approved set shows two related Team C capital-shape dialects that must still feel like one family. First: the low slab-lance shield-cruiser capitals used by Transport, Carrier Support, Bulwark, and the Mothership. These are extremely long and low, often roughly three to four times as long as they are tall, with layered deck terraces, stacked stern citadels, broad shoulder sponsons, and long elegant spear or capsule noses wrapped around luminous centerline glazing. Second: the sanctum-wing capitals used by Vanguard, Command and Intel, Boarding and Recovery, Interdiction, Shield Bastion, Fleet Teleporter, Elite Supership Command, and Hyperweapon. These are taller, usually closer to two to one, with diamond or sail-like shoulder masses, protected central naves, stained-glass shield fields, and long forward sanctum bodies projecting from a broad ceremonial power core. The Mobile Station is its own harbor variant with huge service bays, a long exposed spinal channel, and a crescent bow cradle. The Artillery Titan sits between the families as a long weapon-body attached to a fat rear shoulder block.

Keep every ship as one clean contiguous super-capital silhouette with strong bilateral symmetry. Favor long spearhead or rounded capsule noses, broad shoulder masses, narrow or protected central spines, integrated shield-lattice structures, and glazing fields set into the hull instead of painted on top. Surface treatment should feel like pale celadon ceramic-metal, mint-tinted alloy, polished sea-green armor, brushed silver-green plating, and deep emerald or teal shield windows, channel cuts, nave glazing, and luminous lattice ribs. Let the green live in translucent panes, long window bands, and shield-sail insets rather than cobalt blocks or blue navy striping. Avoid blue-allied paint language, weathered gray steel dominance, rugged industrial bunker massing, detached floating rings, exposed gun barrels, visible missiles, aircraft wings, mech legs, little escort craft, or clutter. The green Mothership should be the brightest, calmest, widest, and most authoritative expression of this same shield-warship family.

## Missile Team D / Viper Barrage Syndicate

Use the visual language of an armored missile fleet built like mobile bunkers, not sleek warships. Team D ships should feel brutally overbuilt, slow, and stubborn, with their survival coming from absurd physical armor thickness instead of shields, agility, or elegant technology. The family language should come from large slab shoulders, deep inset machinery trenches, heavy layered side armor, blunt prow geometry, and long keel-like center bodies that read as armored industrial citadels. Think fortress destroyers, missile bastions, and convoy killers shaped by armor blocks and internal launch magazines rather than by aerodynamic or naval grace.

The strongest silhouette cue is a broad armored upper body with weight concentrated in thick shoulder masses, ribbed dorsal machinery blocks, and a narrow or tapered lower keel that still feels dense and plated rather than sleek. Use large planar armor panels, inset maintenance channels, recessed mechanical cavities, bolted shells, stepped structural blocks, protected vent farms, and heavy central spine casings. Hidden missile capacity should be implied through mass, magazine depth, blast-shielded recesses, and armored casket-like compartments, never through visible missiles or external launch racks.

These are Titan and Mothership hulls, so every silhouette should read as a super-capital ship first. Dominant materials should be matte blackened steel, soot-dark armor, oxidized brown plating, burnt bronze, charred ceramic, and dirty industrial metal with worn rust-like abrasion. Surface treatment should feel thick, old, scarred, and practical. Avoid shield language entirely: no halos, no glow-strip elegance, no luminous seam networks, no smooth energy curves, no glassy advanced-tech surfaces, no sharp fighter noses, and no conventional arrowhead navy styling.

The Titans should look like branches of one bunker-fleet family: same overbuilt armor logic, same industrial brutality, same hidden-salvo menace, but with distinct operational roles. The Team D Mothership should be the apex of the family: part armored citadel, part missile harbor, part brutal logistics bastion, unmistakably larger and more important than any Titan.
