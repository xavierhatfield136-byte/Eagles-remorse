# Master Faction Descriptions

Use these as copy-paste faction preambles before the per-ship detail line when generating ship hulls in ChatGPT.

Suggested order:
1. Start with your shared base sprite prompt.
2. Paste one faction block from this file.
3. Add the ship-specific detail line from `HULL_PROMPTS.md` or a Titan prompt file.

## Shared Base Prompt

```text
Top-down orthographic 2D starship hull concept for a game sprite, single ship only, hull only, no turrets, no guns, no missile pods, no hardpoints, no exposed weapons, no antennas, no text, no insignia, no crew, no background, transparent background, centered, nose pointing right, clean alpha silhouette, readable at small size, flat painted albedo, crisp panel lines, no shadows outside the hull, no side view, no perspective distortion, strong faction identity through hull shape alone.
```

## Blue / Player / Ally

```text
Use the visual language of a heroic blue allied expeditionary navy. These ships should feel human-made, disciplined, and militarized, with strong central spines, broad armored shoulders, practical command-deck language, layered prow geometry, and clean continuous silhouettes that read immediately as real warships from far away. The faction should feel prestigious and dependable rather than alien, ornate, or civilian.

Dominant materials should be cool naval steel, pale gray plating, slate-blue and cobalt armor fields, restrained navy fleet markings, and small cyan command or sensor lights. Blue should read as painted armor and identification panels, not as glowing energy seams across the whole hull. Keep the main hull body mostly steel and gray, with blue concentrated on armor belts, command sections, recessed stripe panels, and selected hull blocks.

Avoid airplane wings, jet fuselages, tailfins, anime mecha shapes, raised cockpit canopies, detached floating parts, and giant exposed cannons. Weapon identity should be implied through hull architecture and military massing rather than visible guns. Even smaller craft should still read as compact warships from a disciplined fleet, not as atmospheric aircraft or shuttles.
```

## Red / Enemy / Team B

```text
Use the visual language of a disciplined red kinetic navy that believes wars are won by armor, gunnery, and command pressure rather than spectacle. These ships should feel practical, militarized, and severe, with dense armored spines, broad shoulders, layered prow geometry, hard centerline mass, and straightforward line-battle warship silhouettes. The faction should feel rugged, authoritative, and conventional in a dangerous way.

Dominant materials should be blackened steel, dark gunmetal, charcoal armor, deep crimson identification fields, restrained warning-stripe reds, and small practical white or amber utility lights. Red should read as painted armor sectors and tactical markings, not as bright glowing seams. Keep the hull body mostly dark steel and iron, with red concentrated on command zones, armor blocks, panel belts, and military ID sections.

Avoid airplane styling, detached parts, smooth luxury forms, exaggerated sci-fi curves, or giant visible cannons. Weapon identity should come from armored mass, prow shape, spinal structure, and siege-like hull architecture rather than external guns or missiles. Even smaller ships should look like honest military hardware built by a fleet that values pressure, durability, and line combat.
```

## Green / Team C / Aegis Lattice

```text
Use the visual language of the existing Team C green fleet already established in the project. These ships are not blue allied hulls recolored green, and they are not stark white cathedral ships. They should read as bright pale jade, celadon, sea-glass, and silver-green military hulls with luminous emerald-teal shield glazing embedded into smooth armor. The overall impression should be brighter and greener than the blue faction, with broad luminous midtones and only limited dark recesses.

Build the silhouette as a long, low, horizontally stretched shield warship. Most medium and large hulls should read as wide 2:1 planforms: calm continuous hull bodies, long spearhead or wedge noses, broad shoulder masses, narrow central spines, smooth side arcs, and very few detached sub-forms. Keep one clean contiguous hull, strong bilateral symmetry, and integrated shield-lattice shoulder structures. The family should feel precise, elegant, and line-of-battle oriented, not rugged, blocky, industrial, or bunker-like.

Surface treatment should feel like pale celadon ceramic-metal, mint-tinted alloy, polished sea-green armor, brushed silver-green plating, and deep emerald or teal window bands embedded within the hull itself. Let the green live in translucent shield glazing, channel cuts, command slits, and luminous lattice ribs rather than painted cobalt blocks or blue navy striping. Avoid weathered gunmetal dominance, cobalt or navy panels, industrial trenching, exposed weapons, aircraft wings, raised cockpits, detached fins, or clutter. Even smaller craft should still feel like miniature shield warships descended from the same family.
```

## Yellow / Team D / Viper Barrage Syndicate

```text
Use the visual language of an armored missile fleet built like mobile bunkers, not sleek warships. These ships should feel brutally overbuilt, slow, and stubborn, with their survival coming from absurd physical armor thickness, layered shell protection, and internal magazine mass instead of shields, agility, or elegant technology. The faction should feel industrial, oppressive, and dangerous in bursts.

Build the silhouette from large slab shoulders, blunt prow geometry, deep inset machinery trenches, long keel-like center bodies, stepped armor blocks, ribbed superstructure casings, and broad armored upper masses. Hidden missile capacity should be implied through casket-like shell sections, blast-shielded recesses, and heavy structural volume, never through visible missiles or external launch racks.

Dominant materials should be matte blackened steel, soot-dark armor, oxidized brown plating, burnt bronze, charred ceramic, dirty industrial metal, and deeply buried amber utility lights. Avoid shield language, elegant glow-strip seams, smooth advanced-tech curves, airplane noses, and conventional navy arrowheads. Even small ships should look like compact armored bunker-craft rather than fighters or shuttles.
```
