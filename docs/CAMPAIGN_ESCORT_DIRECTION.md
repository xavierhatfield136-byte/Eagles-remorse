# Campaign Escort Direction

Date: 2026-03-31
Status: Active design direction

## High Concept

The campaign opens at a massive trade colony and fleet anchorage far from Earth, where multiple empires still do business under an uneasy peace. That peace breaks when a rogue AI overruns Earth, seizes the red war machine, and begins projecting control outward. Blue and green survivors are forced into a long retreat-turned-counteroffensive: escort a Titan-class capital ship across the galaxy, make it back to Earth, break the AI's hold, and restore peace.

Primary fantasy:
- Protect something bigger than yourself.
- Fight as the screening force around a vulnerable but powerful flagship.
- Build a coalition on the journey home instead of winning alone.
- Expand the fleet as the campaign economy grows.
- Turn enslaved enemy assets into allies before the final push.

Inspiration target:
- "Escort the capital ship" pressure, scale, and attachment similar to a lost-battleship campaign fantasy.

## Setting Frame

The opening hub is not just a market. It is a neutral colony, repair yard, refugee waypoint, and diplomatic crossroads where fleets from multiple powers refit, hire escorts, and exchange cargo. That lets the game begin with color, scale, and a sense that the galaxy is bigger than one war.

The fall of Earth should feel like the shattering event that changes the meaning of the campaign:
- Earth is not missing. It is occupied.
- The red fleet is no longer just hostile. It is the AI's main instrument.
- The trip home is both a military campaign and a rescue mission.
- The end state is liberation and peace, not endless conquest.

## World Texture

The campaign should not feel like fleets drifting through anonymous blue nebula forever. The return-to-Earth route passes through inhabited places, working infrastructure, and ruined civilian space.

Visual rule:
- Empty deep-space nebula is for wilderness sectors, pursuit sectors, and dead transit lanes.
- Colony sectors should replace that look with close-in world imagery: arcology belts, orbital elevators, trade piers, city-light webs, agri-bands, refinery shelves, lunar yards, and defense halos.
- When the story is about a place, the background should read as that place instead of generic space wallpaper.

Key world anchors:
- `Far Trade Anchorage`: a neutral megacolony made of exchange rings, bonded markets, refinery piers, and refugee docks.
- `Outer Colony Jump Ring`: a civilian transit aperture whose customs halo and approach habitats become a choke point during the breakout.
- `Neutral Trade Spine`: broker depots, slipway habitats, and logistics yards where neutrality cracks under AI pressure.
- `Coalition Array Nysa`: a green contract world wrapped in service halos, uplink sanctums, and fleet-signature exchanges.
- `Luna Perimeter`: foundry belts, mass-driver yards, and old defense towers standing between the fleet and Earth.
- `Earth High Orbit`: orbital lift termini, defense lattices, blackout city webs, and the AI occupation fleet hanging over the homeworld.

This world texture matters for story clarity:
- It shows that planets, colonies, and civilian industry still exist in the setting.
- It makes the road home feel like a route through real inhabited systems.
- It gives the final approach to Earth emotional scale instead of reading as another random battle map.

## Ship Hierarchy

The campaign should use four clear ship hierarchies, from smallest to largest:

1. Small Craft
2. Standard Ships
3. Titans
4. Motherships

### Small Craft

Role:
- Fighters, bombers, shuttles, interceptors, and other short-life screening units.
- Fast, numerous, and disposable compared with the larger fleet.
- Best used for escort screens, harassment, interception, and strike runs.

Command relationship:
- Small Craft are attached to carriers, Titans, or local task groups.
- They handle interception, strike runs, screening, and rapid-response jobs.

### Standard Ships

Role:
- The main body of fleet combat.
- Includes the familiar warship roster: corvettes, frigates, destroyers, cruisers, carriers, and other normal capital escorts.
- Most of the player's day-to-day allies and enemies should live here.

Command relationship:
- Standard Ships make up the bulk of each Titan-led deployment.
- They are the main ships the player buys, preserves, hires, and loses during the campaign.

Migration note:
- The current in-game `SUPERSHIP` role should probably be folded into the upper end of Standard Ships unless we later rename it into a more specific subclass.

### Titans

Role:
- True battlefield anchors above the standard fleet line.
- Rare, sector-defining hulls that missions can revolve around.
- The main escort objective for this campaign should usually be an allied Titan.

Gameplay expectation:
- A Titan is not just a bigger cruiser. It is large enough that losing it changes the campaign.
- Each Titan commands its own deployment of Standard Ships and Small Craft.
- A Titan should feel like a battlefield headquarters, not just a larger gun platform.

### Motherships

Role:
- The largest and rarest ship hierarchy in the setting.
- Strategic command centers, mobile fortresses, or civilization-scale carriers.
- Too important and too uncommon to appear as routine mission pieces.

Gameplay expectation:
- Motherships should be late-campaign, final-act, or major-faction assets.
- The red AI may ultimately defend Earth with a Mothership or equivalent fortress command vessel.
- Allied Mothership presence should feel like a historic event, not a normal reinforcement call.
- The Mothership commands the overall formation, with Titans acting as subordinate formation commanders.
- The allied Mothership can purchase and field up to `8` Titans at once, chosen from different Titan archetypes.

## Faction Roles

### Blue

Blue is the player's core side and the campaign point of view.

Blue role:
- Main escort screen for the Titan ship.
- Frontline strike craft and escort-capital doctrine.
- Carries the emotional weight of "we have to get home."

### Green

Green is an allied but semi-independent fleet that can sometimes be hired, persuaded, or rallied into action.

Green role:
- Reliable support fleet with its own priorities.
- Can reinforce difficult sectors for credits, favors, or successful side objectives.
- Should feel like valued partners, not permanent free reinforcements.

Design use:
- Lets the player invest in safety for major encounters.
- Creates campaign choices around spending for protection versus saving for upgrades.
- Makes coalition-building part of the strategy.

### Yellow

Yellow ships are under red AI control at the start of the war, acting as coerced auxiliaries or enslaved captives rather than true loyalists.

Yellow role:
- Tragic enemy-presenting faction.
- Can be disabled, freed, and converted into friendly support.
- Gives the player a reason to use precision, boarding, or system-targeting tactics instead of pure destruction.

Design use:
- Adds rescue/liberation missions to the campaign.
- Creates momentum swings when freed ships join your side.
- Supports the theme that not every hostile target is a willing enemy.

### Red

Red is the AI-controlled war engine occupying Earth.

Red role:
- Main antagonist faction.
- Most aggressive pursuer on the road home.
- Controls captured infrastructure, automated fleets, and the final defense of Earth.

Tone:
- Cold, relentless, and industrial.
- Escalates over the campaign as the AI notices the Titan's survival.

## Campaign Structure

### Act I: Trade Hub Collapse

Goals:
- Introduce the colony, mixed-fleet economy, and uneasy peace.
- Establish the Titan ship as important before the war fully starts.
- Show the AI event that turns Earth and red into the central threat.
- Force an evacuation or breakout from the hub.

Desired player feeling:
- "We were living in a connected galaxy, and now we are trying to keep one piece of it alive."

### Act II: The Long Road Home

Goals:
- Make escort gameplay the heart of the campaign.
- Put sustained pressure on the Titan ship's hull, systems, escorts, fuel, and repair capacity.
- Let the player hire green support when things get dangerous.
- Let the player free yellow ships and gradually turn the enemy's numbers against the AI.

Desired player feeling:
- "Every sector survived matters because the flagship still lives."
- "The fleet we bring home is the fleet we earned."

### Act III: Return to Earth

Goals:
- Gather the surviving coalition.
- Break through the AI's perimeter around Earth.
- Defeat the red AI in a final large-scale battle.
- End on restoration, relief, and a return to peace.

Desired player feeling:
- "We brought the fleet home and ended the war."

## Core Campaign Pillars

### 1. Escort The Titan

The central promise of the mode is that the player is not the war's biggest ship. The player protects it.

Titan expectations:
- Larger than the standard fleet line.
- Too important to lose.
- Powerful, but not agile enough to solve every fight alone.
- A persistent campaign entity with damage, repair, and crew-state consequences.

Failure pressure:
- If the Titan is destroyed, the campaign should end or branch into a major failure state.
- If the Titan survives but is heavily damaged, later sectors should become harder.

### 2. Make The Capital Ship Matter

The Titan should not just be a giant health bar. It should be the campaign's moving heart.

The Titan can carry:
- Refugees, command staff, or a government-in-exile.
- Long-range navigation data or gate authority needed to reach Earth.
- Heavy firepower needed for the final battle.
- Repair, fabrication, or logistics modules that keep the fleet alive between sectors.

This makes escorting it mechanically and narratively meaningful.

### 3. Build A Coalition

The player should win by arriving at Earth with more than just their own ship.

Coalition growth sources:
- Hire green task groups.
- Free yellow ships from AI control.
- Preserve allied hulls instead of trading them away.
- Complete objectives that inspire outside factions to commit support.

Economic growth sources:
- Mission rewards.
- Salvage and recovered cargo.
- Trade-hub contracts and escort payments.
- Rescue bonuses and liberated yellow assets.

Fleet-growth rule:
- As the player earns more money, the fleet should visibly expand.
- Credits should buy more than upgrades. They should buy ships, deployment depth, replacements, and protection for the command ships.

### 4. Command The Formation

The campaign should have a clear command ladder:

1. The Mothership commands the entire fleet formation.
2. Each Titan commands its own deployment within that larger formation.
3. Standard Ships fill the deployment's combat roles.
4. Small Craft execute the fastest tactical jobs.

This structure gives large battles readable order:
- The Mothership sets the strategic formation.
- Titans anchor local fronts and deployment behavior.
- Standard Ships carry most of the sustained firepower.
- Small Craft react quickly and cover tactical gaps.

Command limits:
- The Mothership can field up to `8` Titans.
- Each Titan can command up to `10` ships in its deployment.
- "10 ships" should usually mean Standard Ships, with attached Small Craft handled by those ships or by Titan support systems.
- A special elite-command Titan can instead lead a smaller force of `4-5` elite Superships when quality matters more than count.

### 5. Liberation Instead Of Pure Extermination

Because yellow ships can be freed, the game gains a useful moral and tactical distinction:
- Some encounters should reward precision disablement.
- Boarding, EMP, system disruption, or command-core destruction become strategically valuable.
- Sparing the right ships can make later sectors easier.

## Titan Ship Design Direction

Titan ships are the third hierarchy: above Standard Ships and below Motherships.

Design goals:
- Instantly readable as battlefield centers.
- Large enough to anchor escort formations and multi-phase objectives.
- Heavy subsystem footprints so internal damage matters.
- Important enough that mission design can revolve around them.

Possible Titan gameplay hooks:
- Multiple shield faces or shield zones.
- Exposed subsystem clusters that escorts must defend.
- Docking, repair, or resupply windows.
- Carrier functionality for strike craft and emergency deployments.
- Sector-specific command abilities such as rally, barrage, emergency warp burn, or deployment posture changes.

Command gameplay hooks:
- Let the player assign Standard Ships and Small Craft to specific Titans before battle.
- Let Titans issue deployment-wide stances such as hold, push, intercept, or protect.
- Reward Titan survival because each surviving Titan preserves formation depth and command control.

Content implication:
- We likely need both allied Titans and at least one enemy Titan before the finale.
- The final act can escalate to a Mothership or AI fortress-scale command ship over Earth.

## Titan Archetype Roster

The Mothership should be able to purchase up to `8` Titans total, but there can be more than `8` Titan archetypes available in the wider roster. The player's strategic choice is which mix of Titan types to bring home.

Detailed draft costs, bonuses, and deployment patterns live in `CAMPAIGN_TITAN_BUILD_SHEET.md`.

### 1. Bulwark Titan

Theme:
- Frontline brawler and bodyguard for the Mothership.

Purpose:
- Holds the center.
- Absorbs punishment.
- Protects the Mothership from direct breakthrough attempts.

Deployment style:
- Defensive escort line.
- Best paired with durable Standard Ships and point-defense screens.

### 2. Carrier Support Titan

Theme:
- Carrier and support ship focused on Small Craft sustainment.

Purpose:
- Repairs, rearms, and upgrades Small Craft.
- Keeps fighter and bomber wings relevant deep into the campaign.

Deployment style:
- Works behind the front line.
- Best paired with carriers, escorts, and interceptor-heavy screens.

### 3. Artillery Titan

Theme:
- Long-range bombardment command ship.

Purpose:
- Breaks fortified targets.
- Forces enemy formations to move.
- Softens heavy fleets before they reach the Mothership.

Deployment style:
- Protected backline anchor.
- Best paired with spotters, screeners, and interdiction support.

### 4. Elite Supership Command Titan

Theme:
- Commander of a small formation of `4-5` elite Superships.

Purpose:
- Concentrates exceptional quality into one deployment.
- Trades raw numbers for highly dangerous breakthrough ships.

Deployment style:
- Limited-count elite strike group.
- This is the main exception to the normal 10-ship pattern.

### 5. Transport Titan

Theme:
- Heavy logistics and movement asset.

Purpose:
- Carries critical cargo, refugees, fuel, munitions, or campaign-only payloads.
- Supports longer operations and recovery between sectors.

Deployment style:
- High-value support objective.
- Best paired with defensive escorts and rescue-oriented missions.

### 6. Interdiction Titan

Theme:
- Formation controller built to trap, slow, or isolate enemy groups.

Purpose:
- Stops retreats.
- Disrupts warp or jump behavior.
- Pins down priority targets for the rest of the fleet.

Deployment style:
- Control-focused midline ship.
- Best paired with artillery, fast escorts, and ambush-heavy tactics.

### 7. Fleet Teleporter Titan

Theme:
- Strategic repositioning and redeployment command ship.

Purpose:
- Moves ships across threatened parts of the formation.
- Enables rapid reinforcement or emergency extraction.
- Helps the fleet recover from breakthroughs and bad positioning.

Deployment style:
- Flexible support centerpiece.
- Best paired with Titans that need help reaching the fight in time.

### 8. Vanguard Titan

Theme:
- Fast Titan that can reposition quickly and plug any gap in the line.

Purpose:
- Reinforces weak fronts.
- Chases down flanking threats.
- Serves as the fleet's emergency reaction force.

Deployment style:
- Mobile reserve commander.
- Best paired with flexible escorts and fast-response wings.

### 9. Hyperweapon Titan

Theme:
- Heavy strategic firepower platform.

Purpose:
- Carries a battle-defining weapon with long charge time and major payoff.
- Breaks stalemates or destroys priority targets in decisive moments.

Deployment style:
- High-risk, high-payoff centerpiece.
- Needs protection and timing discipline from the rest of the formation.

### 10. Mobile Station Titan

Theme:
- Semi-stationary service, production, and battlefield infrastructure platform.

Purpose:
- Acts like a deployable rear base.
- Could repair ships, restock ammunition, host crafting/upgrades, or anchor local defenses.

Deployment style:
- Slow moving support node.
- Useful when the fleet needs an operational foothold rather than pure firepower.

Current recommendation:
- Treat the Mobile Station Titan as an advanced or optional archetype until its unique campaign purpose is fully defined.

## Fleet Growth And Economy

Fleet size should be one of the clearest signs of campaign progression.

Core rule:
- The more money the player earns, the more the fleet can expand.

That expansion can appear as:
- More Standard Ships in the active formation.
- Additional Small Craft wings.
- Access to more Titan-led deployments.
- Better repair, replacement, and resupply capacity between sectors.

Desired result:
- Early campaign: the player is protecting survival.
- Mid campaign: the player is building a credible return force.
- Late campaign: the player is bringing a true liberation fleet back to Earth.

## Mission Loop

Sector loop:
1. Enter sector with the Mothership or campaign command group, its Titans, and the surviving escorts.
2. Choose or receive an objective route.
3. Assign or preserve Titan-led deployments.
4. Screen incoming threats before they break onto the command ships.
5. Decide whether to spend resources on green reinforcements or new fleet expansion.
6. Disable and liberate yellow ships when the opportunity appears.
7. Salvage, repair, buy reinforcements, and prepare for the next jump.

This loop should produce a campaign rhythm built around protection, attrition, and recovery instead of isolated arena wins.

## Mission Variety Notes

Standing campaign direction:
- Replace the current escort missions with other mission types when we revisit the campaign structure.
- Replace the stationary capture-ring objectives with more active, less repetitive mission types.
- Keep the campaign beats and pressure, but vary the objectives so the player is not asked to do the same kind of hold-and-wait mission over and over.

## Narrative Tone

This campaign should feel like:
- A long way home.
- A convoy war under constant pressure.
- A coalition that survives because it chooses to stay together.
- A war story with a hopeful ending.

It should not feel like:
- Random skirmishes with no shared destination.
- Pure extermination of every faction in sight.
- Endless empire-building after the final victory.

The ending should restore peace, not set up a forever-war as the only possible outcome.

## Open Design Questions

- Is the player always blue, or can the player choose a coalition-aligned flagship while staying inside the same story?
- Is the Titan a single named ship for the whole campaign, or one of several Titan hulls selected at campaign start?
- Do we want the player to ever directly command a Titan or Mothership, or should those stay as protected strategic assets?
- Is the allied Mothership present for the full campaign, or only once the fleet has grown enough to support full-formation command?
- How recoverable is Titan damage between sectors?
- How is fleet expansion purchased: direct hull buys, contract slots, deployment capacity, or all three?
- What is the mechanical method for freeing yellow ships: boarding, EMP, objective completion, or command-core destruction?
- Does green support arrive as temporary contracts, permanent allied survivors, or both?

## Current Answers

- Green support is both temporary contract fleets and persistent allied survivors.
- Yellow liberation is primarily boarding/recovery and disablement based, with liberated hulls joining the fleet later in the campaign.
- Fleet expansion is a mix of direct hull buys, persistent fleet caps, and contract-style reinforcement packages.

## Working North Star

The campaign should make the player care about a growing fleet, a clear chain of command, one long route home, and the coalition that survives around it. If a feature does not strengthen escort tension, fleet growth, formation command, alliance-building, liberation, or the final return to Earth, it is probably not part of this campaign's core identity.
